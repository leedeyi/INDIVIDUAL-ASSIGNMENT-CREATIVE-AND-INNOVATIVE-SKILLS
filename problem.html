Financial crisis during the COVID-19 pandemic

Due to the Perintah Kawalan Pergerakan (PKP), Zoo Negara Malaysia has been forced to completely close its operations to visitors. It leads Zoo Negara faced difficulties in providing food for thousands of animals including elephants, giraffes, and tigers throughout the COVID-19 pandemic. Dwindling funds left the zoo with less than RM1 million in savings, a critical shortage compared to its monthly food budget of RM1.3 million to RM1.5 million. Zoo Negara relies highly on ticket sales, event venue rentals, and private sponsorships. The closure of the premises means a sudden cutoff of financial resources.

Perception of Safety and Security by Visitors at Zoo Negara Malaysia

Social media complaints highlighting inadequate animal barriers, rusty or dirty cages, cracked and uneven walkways have seriously undermined the trust of visitors, resulting in a significant decline in ticket sales. These maintenance issues combined with outdated zoo-style concrete fences leave a negative visual impression on visitors, making them feel unsafe and affecting their future visits. Moreover, empirical research shows that the public emergency exit accessibility, wheelchair usability, and the restrooms floor safety scored the lowest in facility safety indicators, with a mean score of 3.59. Other operational hazards such as algae-covered floors, stagnant water in enclosures after heavy rain, and inadequate signage for first aid equipment which received a mean score of 3.10, further reinforce the public's negative perception of basic hygiene and safety standards. The Covid-19 (years 2020) pandemic happened made these safety and infrastructure problems severely worsened for the Zoo to fix them.

Poor Visitor Experience and Service

During peak hours such as weekends and public holidays, due to the slow physical ticket purchasing process and the limitations in the online ticketing system, causing a severe congestion at the entrance and long queues. In addition, the internal transportation service encountered a problem where the limited tram fleet led to longer waiting times and inconvenience to tourists especially families with young children or the elderly. On top of this, the food and beverages offerings within the premises were rather unsatisfactory, limiting the dining options and the prices were generally considered not matching of the quality of food provided, resulting in an overall poor experience.

Enclosure Hygiene, Animal Welfare, and Visual Hygiene at Zoo Negara Malaysia

Public reports and online reviews indicate that unsatisfactory enclosure cleanliness such as murky water, uncleaned viewing glass, and substandard living conditions for aquatic species that negatively impacts visitor perceptions. Furthermore, a visitor noted that they hardly saw any staff or workers implementing standard operating procedures (SOPs) at the entrance, which further damages the overall visitor experience. This is because the zoo's condition directly represents Malaysia's image on the global stage to international tourists. Ultimately, when visitors find cleanliness and management levels inadequate, it undermines their trust in the zoo's operations and raises greater concerns regarding safety and general facility maintenance.
